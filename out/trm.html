AI Reference Catalog & Realization

Two registers kept apart on purpose. The reference catalog says what could exist in the AI domain — services, building blocks, patterns and standards, vendor-neutral and stable. The realization catalog says what this institution actually provides, as capability × pattern × technology, each carrying a readiness level. Readiness is never merged with capability maturity. A capability carries maturity. A realization carries readiness. They measure different things and are never combined into a single number. A realization is one row per capability × pattern × technology — a technology appears on as many rows as it realizes patterns, which is correct and which a two-tier model cannot express. Never write a readiness level into the capability catalog: "semantic retrieval, readiness 4 via Azure AI Search" is a true statement about supply that silently substitutes for the maturity question.

What actually backs this layer, assessed rather than asserted. The honest finding: no standards body publishes a functional taxonomy of AI services. ISO 42001, 5338, 23894 and NIST AI RMF — the backbone of the capability model — contain none. What exists instead is a set of protocol specifications, community taxonomies and vendor decompositions of varying rigour, listed here with their real status.

Six objects. An earlier version of this model had three, and defined an SBB as "the product you run, with a SKU". TOGAF says the opposite — §33.2.4: an SBB "may be either procured or developed". Your Terraform modules are an SBB: developed, in-house, reusable. And TOGAF has no formal "product" concept at all, so technology offering and deployed instance are fields on the SBB record here, not model objects — a local convention, stated as one.

TOGAF does not prescribe the ordering of capability, pattern and building block. Pattern is not even a formal entity in the content metamodel, and the text says patterns identify combinations of "ABBs and/or SBBs". The chain above is a stated local convention, not a standard we can cite. That is worth knowing: no amount of further reading settles it, so it is settled by decision and recorded here.

A pattern, written properly

Four of the five patterns in the catalog are names with a one-line description — candidates, not patterns. TOGAF's template requires problem, context, forces, solution, resulting context, rationale and known uses. Here is one done to that standard, as the worked example.
