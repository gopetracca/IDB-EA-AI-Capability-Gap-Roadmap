1
The problem

Two people, opposite answers, both right

“We cannot run AI agents.”

“Of course we can — the platform, the standards, the reference architectures and the infrastructure modules are all published.”

Both are true, about different things. One describes whether the work is done. The other describes whether the means to do it exist.

A model that answers with a single number has to pick one of those meanings, and whichever it picks, the other conversation loses its evidence. That is the standard failure of AI capability assessment: supply gets reported as ability, because supply is easier to establish and pleasanter to report.

This model is built so both statements can be true at once, visibly, with the evidence attached to each.

2
What it is

Three things, kept apart

A capability map

8 domains, 56 capabilities, 283 criteria. What the institution must be able to do with AI, each with one accountable owner. Vendor-neutral: it survives replacing every product.

A register of what exists

7 offerings and 20 named assets — standards, reference architectures, templates, modules — each with a status and a location a reader can open. This part is not an opinion.

Four observations

Recorded as facts, with evidence, an observer and a date. A level is derived from them by a written rule. Nobody types a level anywhere.

The separation between the three is the whole design. An observation is a fact, not a score, so several scales can read one body of evidence and none of them can contradict another about what is true — only about what to make of it.

The taxonomy

Three levels, and only one of them is assessed

Count What it is Carries a level Carries an owner
L1 · Domain 8 A reporting cluster. Groups capabilities so a reader can find them no no
L2 · Capability 56 Something the institution must be able to do. The unit of assessment and of accountability yes yes
L3 · Criterion 283 A specific practice that can actually be witnessed on a real system no no

A domain is a reporting cluster

Not a lifecycle. It implies no sequence, no team and no process. Every capability has exactly one primary home; a second plausible home is a typed dependency, not a second listing.

A criterion is a checklist item

Not a gate. Criteria carry an observation, which is a fact. None has a rubric, and none is typed mandatory or conditional.

7
How it measures

Four observations, each one a fact

The question Asked at Evidence expected
Practised Is this specific practice done on real AI systems in production, repeatedly? each L3 criterion Named AI systems or agents where this criterion was done, and by whom
Enabled Can a team get the tooling for this without building it themselves? the capability An offering in the offerings register, or the enterprise service that provides it
Skilled Do the people who must do this know how? the capability Named practitioners, training records, or a competency statement
Defined Is there an approved institutional standard, policy or method for this? Set by whoever owns the subject - the platform team, Cybersecurity, Data Management, Legal, HR or EA. the capability The document, its owner, its location and a date

Each is answered yes / partial / no / n/a / unknown, and each carries evidence, an observer and a date. That is the entire input to the model: 451 recorded observations.

Nobody is being scored. There is no level in the workbook for a reviewer to defend. The level is computed afterwards, from what they recorded, by a rule they can read and argue with separately.

8
How it measures

Where each answer is recorded

The question most often got wrong. Only two of the three taxonomy levels ever carry an observation, and one of the four values is not recorded by anybody — it is computed.

Taxonomy level What is recorded against it Rows
L1 domain · 8 Nothing. A domain is a reporting cluster and is never scored —
L2 capability · 56 enabled, skilled and defined — one row each. Plus practised, derived from the criteria beneath it and never typed 168
L3 criterion · 283 practised — one row per criterion, because that is the level at which work can actually be witnessed 283

A round collects 451 rows, not 56. And the capability-level practised value every chart is drawn from was computed by the roll-up: there is nowhere to type it, and a stored level is the one edit validation rejects outright.

9
How it measures

Five values, and the two that get misused

Value Means Requires
yes Done Evidence
partial Done in places, or incompletely. Say where Evidence
no An evidenced negative. Someone looked and it is not there Evidence, or a basis saying what was looked at
n/a Does not apply here. A governance capability is not worse for having no tooling A recorded reason
unknown Nobody has looked. Never a zero Nothing. It is the honest default

no and unknown are the pair that decides whether the model is trustworthy. A register that was searched and had nothing is unknown until the owner is asked — a standard owned by another function may exist and simply not be recorded here. Recording that absence as no turns we did not look into it does not exist, which is the failure this model exists to prevent.

Most published models cannot say unknown at all, so they silently score an unexamined capability as if it were absent.

10
How it measures

Why practice is observed one level down

4.5 Integration & Tool Enablement covers 6 distinct practices. The Bank might do one of them well and never do another at all — so “is 4.5 practised?” has no honest single answer, and is not asked.

L3 Criterion The practice
4.5.1 MCP & System Integration Design Design integration to institutional systems with contracts and error semantics.
4.5.2 MCP Tool & Function Definition Define the callable actions exposed to a model, with typed inputs and stated effects.
4.5.3 Capability Exposure to AI Clients Expose institutional capability to AI clients through the prevailing open tool-interface standard.
4.5.4 Identity Propagation & Delegated Access Carry the acting user's identity through to the system of record.
4.5.5 Legacy & Core System Adaptation Reach systems that were never designed to be called by an AI client.
4.5.6 Agent-to-Agent Interoperability Publish and consume agent descriptions, negotiate a task through a defined lifecycle, and carry identity and authorization across an agent boundary.

Each criterion is judged on its own, and the capability value is derived. The gain is that a gap gets a name: not “improve integration & tool enablement” but a specific practice nobody has done, which is a piece of work someone can be given.

11
How it measures

The roll-up, and why it leans one way

The criteria say The capability reads
Every one examined, and every one yes yes
Some yes or partial, or any left unexamined partial
None yes or partial, at least one no no
None examined unknown

One weak link stops the claim

A capability cannot read yes on the strength of one observation while the rest has never been looked at. An unexamined criterion is never counted as satisfied.

The asymmetry is deliberate

yes needs a complete look. no does not: one examined no with the rest unexamined still reads no. Under-claiming costs a follow-up question. Over-claiming is the failure the model exists to prevent.

12
How it measures

A level is derived, never typed

Has the institution established this practice?

Level Name What it means Derivable today
0 Incomplete The capability is not performed, or performance cannot be shown. yes
1 Performed It is done on real AI systems. Nothing is guaranteed to be repeatable. yes
2 Managed It is done, the tooling is provided, and the people doing it are competent. yes
3 Established An approved institutional standard exists and the work is done against it. yes
4 Predictable Performance is measured against thresholds and held there. —
5 Innovating One evidence-driven improvement cycle has closed with a verified benefit. —

Performance comes first, and that ordering is not ours. An approved standard with nothing performed against it earns no level at all — because the question was never did we write it down, it was can the institution do this. That is what stops “we approved the technology” from reading as “we have the capability”.

Level 3 Established is the ceiling of what is measured, not of the scale. The levels above it need observations nobody collects — threshold monitoring and a closed improvement cycle.

13
How it measures

Why a 2 and not a 3

Read each row as to reach at least this level. Nothing here is written down: it is derived by running the rule over all 625 combinations of the five values across the four observations.

To reach Practised Enabled Skilled Defined
0 Incomplete yes or partial or no any any any
1 Performed yes or partial any any any
2 Managed yes yes or n/a yes any
3 Established yes yes or n/a yes yes

The whole difference between 2 Managed and 3 Established is one observation: defined must be yes. Everything else is already required at 2 — which is why a capability with the work done, the tooling provided and competent people stops at 2 until an approved standard exists.

n/a counts as satisfied — a capability that legitimately needs no tooling is not held down for having none. Every row is exact: these conditions are not a summary of the rule, they are the rule.

14
Provenance

Where the scale comes from

Adapted from ISO/IEC 33020:2019 (process measurement framework). Simplified: four observations rather than five process attributes, three values rather than the standard's four-point N-P-L-F scale.

What is adopted

The level ladder, the ordering of the attributes, and the idea that a level is derived from attribute achievement rather than asserted.

What is ours, stated plainly

Collapsing five process attributes to four capability observations; a three-value scale in place of the standard's four-point one; and applying a process measurement framework to a capability map.

Two things must not be quoted. The standard is paywalled and its published preview stops short, so its percentage bands and its exact level rule rest on secondary sources about a superseded predecessor. Cite the scale by name and the levels by clause; never quote the percentages.

Where the model adopts, adapts or invents, it says so on the face of the artifact — every generated view carries this basis line.

15
Several frames

One set of facts, 3 ways to read it

Because an observation is a fact and a level is a rule over facts, more than one rule can read the same evidence. 3 ship, each one file.

Scale Asks Ladder Behaviour Standing
Capability level Has the institution established this practice? 0–5 Innovating gates on practice the default
Executive readiness How much of what we said we would do actually exists? 1–5 Leading does not gate on practice a lens
Institutional maturity How far has this practice spread beyond the people doing it? 1–5 Optimizing gates on practice a lens

Where a lens and the default disagree, the default is the finding. A lens exists so a room that already holds a frame can be answered in it — not so the friendlier number can be chosen.

Adding a scale is one file meeting a published contract. A view is generated for it automatically, and it is validated over all 625 combinations of the five values across the four observations.

18
The finding

The institution has built its enablers ahead of its practice.

That is the explanation for the disagreement we opened with, and it names its own fix. 20 assets recorded with a status and a location; 5 finished but not yet released to teams; and not one observation yet of whether any of it is used.

The distinction the model refuses to blur is between we do not know and we do not have it. Most assessments cannot tell those apart and score an unexamined capability as if it were absent.

20
Starting the assessment

What a round can and cannot say

What a first round can produce

  • A derived level per capability answered, on the default scale and through every lens — from the same evidence.
  • A gap with a name. Not “improve orchestration” but a specific practice nobody has done, which is a piece of work someone can be given.
  • The first honest count of how much of the map has been examined at all.

What it cannot

  • A level above what today's observations reach. The top two need threshold monitoring and a closed improvement cycle, and nothing collects either.
  • Evidence of conformance. A top level reads it from a standard and a practice co-existing; it does not show the work follows the standard.
  • A rating for the 14 capabilities whose taxonomy their owner has not yet agreed.
  • A benchmark. No comparable dataset exists, and inventing one would undo the point.

There is no writing step. build.py all validates the facts and regenerates the workbook and every view, so the report cannot drift from what was recorded — and re-running it after new observations produces a new report rather than a new draft.

22
The rule underneath all of it

Facts are edited. Views are generated. Derived values are never stored.

No finding is ever fixed by editing a report. The fact is fixed, and everything rebuilds — which is why two versions of this model cannot contradict each other about what is true.
facts/ What is true about the Bank, with evidence and a date edited
scales/ Rules that turn observations into a level rarely edited
out/ Views, one per scale, one per question, plus the report generated
23
Appendix · objections, answered

On the ladder

“Why does the top level need a standard? The standard usually comes first — ours did.”
It does, and the model records that. But the ladder orders claims, not activities. The top rung does not claim a standard exists; it claims the work is done against one, and that sentence needs the work. A standard with nothing performed against it is recorded as defined = yes with the practice unobserved — which reads not rated, and is precisely the finding that the enablers were built ahead of the practice. 2 Managed and 3 Established differ by exactly one observation (defined).
“So writing standards earns no credit?”
It earns the credit it should: a visible defined column, an asset register with a status and a location, and an offering a team can actually consume. What it does not earn is a capability level — because letting supply alone place one is how we approved the technology comes to read as we have the capability.
“This ordering is your invention.”
It is not. Performance is the first process attribute in the ISO process measurement framework the default scale is adapted from; a defined process is a Level 3 attribute. We adopted the ordering and say so on every view, along with what is ours.
“Why is almost nothing rated? That makes the model look useless.”
Because the observation every scale needs most has never been collected. 46 of 56 capabilities are unrated for one reason: nobody has been asked whether the work is done. That is a true statement about the assessment, and one review round changes it. Filling those cells with estimates to make the picture look finished is the one change that would make this worthless.
24
Appendix · objections, answered

On the model

“<code>unknown</code> is just a way of avoiding a score.”
It is the opposite — it is the refusal to invent one. no means somebody looked and it is not there, and the record says what they looked at. unknown means nobody looked. Most published models cannot tell those apart and score an unexamined capability as if it were absent, which is how a model starts producing gaps that do not exist.
“56 capabilities is too many. The analyst models use about half that.”
The coarse ones are easier to fill in and produce findings nobody can act on. Granularity is what turns improve orchestration into a named practice with an owner. The 283 criteria beneath the capabilities are where that happens — and they are a checklist behind a judgement, not 283 more things to score.
“This is Enterprise Architecture&rsquo;s view of the Bank.”
EA is the accountable owner of 3 of 56 capabilities. The standard for AI security is Cybersecurity’s, for AI data governance Data Management’s, for literacy Learning & Development’s. A model that only ever said EA would be a credit column, not a model — and 9 capabilities are owned by nobody, which we report rather than quietly assign.
“We already have a maturity model. Why another one?”
Because this is not competing with it. An observation is a fact, so the same evidence can be read through whichever frame a room already holds — 3 scales ship and none of them required reassessing anything. Where a lens and the default disagree, the default is the finding.

Appendix · objections, answered

On the evidence

“You say the top level means we conform to the standard. Prove it.”
We cannot, and the model says so on the face of it. defined says a standard exists; the practice observation says the work is done. Neither says the work follows the standard. The top rung reads conformance from the two facts sitting together and its reason line admits it. A fifth observation would make it checkable; whether that is worth collecting is recorded as an open item, not hidden.
“A governance capability has no tooling. Does it fail?”
No. n/a with a recorded reason drops out of the calculation rather than counting against it. A capability that legitimately needs no platform component is not worse for having none — forcing a supply number onto a strategy capability is how a model starts producing nonsense nobody trusts.
“Can we compare ourselves to peer institutions?”
No, and that is deliberate. No comparable dataset exists at this granularity, and inventing one would undo the point. What the model does instead is make our own position checkable: every observation carries evidence, a name and a date.
“The taxonomy is not agreed yet. Should we be measuring at all?”
Structure is settled ahead of, and separately from, assessment. 14 capabilities carry low confidence and 14 are unverified lenses onto the Bank’s existing map — both listed by name rather than buried. Nothing should be scored against those until their owner has looked, and the anchoring is carved out of any approval request until a crosswalk exists.
