Enterprise AI Capability Model

Fifty capabilities carrying maturity: is the institution able to do this, to what standard, with what evidence? Realizations carry readiness: has the enterprise packaged it well enough to consume? The two scales are never merged, and the gap between them is the finding — a capability can be readiness 4 and maturity 2, and that combination is invisible to any model carrying only one number.

Why one tree and not eight

Every published AI framework is a different dimension, not a competing map. AWS CAF-AI is an organizational perspective. IBM's model is platform realization. ISO/IEC 5338 is lifecycle. NIST AI RMF is risk. Merging them into one hierarchy produces a tree that mixes abilities, phases, assets and products.

This model keeps a single capability spine and attaches everything else as an overlay. The L1 partition follows AWS CAF-AI's perspectives, with Security named explicitly in D7 and three domains inserted in the middle; that is where the shape came from, and per-capability provenance records where the content came from.

Single primary home, not mutual exclusivity

An earlier version of this model claimed the eight domains were mutually exclusive. That claim is withdrawn. Capabilities interact and may serve several value streams; the hierarchy exists to prevent duplication, not to assert ontological exclusivity. Every subject has exactly one primary_domain and may carry supports, depends_on, constrained_by and assured_by links to others.

Five objects, not three

Capability, ABB, SBB, technology offering and deployed instance are five different things. A prior version collapsed the last three, defining an SBB as "the product you run, with a SKU". That is wrong: a TOGAF SBB may be bought or built, and the product is a separate record the SBB uses.

Five matrices follow from this, not two: A capability × ABB, B pattern × ABB, C ABB × SBB, D SBB × offering, E SBB × instance. They are separate because they change on separate clocks — E continuously, D quarterly, A every few years, the capability tree almost never.

The object that fixes the mapping problem

OCR realizes no capability. Speech-to-text realizes no capability. They are offerings in a catalog, and an earlier version mapped them to "Platform Service Catalog Management" as if they realized the ability to manage a catalog — a category error dressed as a fix.

The missing object is the AI product or use case. Capabilities are enabled by ABBs; products consume ABBs and exercise capabilities; the service catalog contains offerings. Matrix A edges are therefore typed — required-enabler, optional-enabler, control-enforcer, evidence-provider, operational-support, catalog-offering — and the ambiguous verb "realizes" between a technical service and an organizational capability is no longer used anywhere.

How a rating is derived

Ordinal maturity scores are not averaged, and this model no longer takes a median or a weakest link of L3 scores. For each subject the rating is gated:

  1. The achieved level is the highest level for which every applicable mandatory criterion at that level and all lower levels is satisfied by valid evidence.
  2. Alternative routes are expressed as approved AND/OR gate logic. Enhancing criteria never compensate for an unmet mandatory gate.
  3. Conditional criteria enter the gate only when a pre-approved trigger is true; applicability is settled before evidence is examined, and versioned with the rubric.
  4. Only a predeclared essential control may cap capability maturity. Other failed controls create conformance gaps and affect assurance, rather than silently lowering the organizational rating.
  5. Never score L1. Domains are reporting containers; a domain average hides the one subject that is failing.

Not rating is a result

Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence. 0 means a documented inquiry positively established that the ability does not operate. Where that inquiry has not happened, the honest answer is a state, not a number:

  • NE — applicable, but evidence is insufficient to rate. Shows in the completeness view.
  • UC — applicability or legal status under clarification. An active decision gap with an owner.
  • NA — formally not applicable, with approver, rationale and expiry.

These are states, not levels. They never enter arithmetic. For an institution whose legal applicability is itself undetermined, UC will be the correct answer more often than any number.

Assessment context

A rating without a context is not a result. Every result belongs to an assessment context — organizational boundary, as-of date, assessor, rubric version, and in a full implementation also jurisdiction, use-case class, risk tier and autonomy level. Local ratings are not averaged into an enterprise rating. Enterprise maturity is assessed separately against enterprise coverage criteria; local results are reported as a distribution.

Four views, never combined

Maturity, evidence sufficiency, gap and repository anchor answer different questions on incompatible scales, so the heat map switches between them rather than fusing them into one RAG. Four more views — risk exposure, control assurance, architecture enablement, adoption and value — belong in the target method but require data that will not exist at a first baseline.

Priority is a governed decision, not a subtraction. A three-level gap can be low priority. Subjects fall into categorical lanes and a Priority Decision record settles each one.

What this model does not yet do

  • No per-subject rubrics. The gated method is defined; the fifty rubrics are not written. Until a subject has one, its rating is provisional and should be labeled as such.
  • L3 criteria are not typed mandatory / conditional / enhancing, so the gates cannot yet be evaluated mechanically.
  • No control mapping. ISO/IEC 42001 Annex A and NIST AI RMF outcomes are not mapped onto subjects. D7 should be carved out of any approval request until they are.
  • Anchors are hypotheses. Every New / Specialization / Lens class carries a confidence and is unverified until the institution's existing capability map is crosswalked.
  • No provenance ledger. Sources are named per subject but not pinned to edition, clause, access date and archive reference. The model is reviewable, not yet reproducible.
  • Legal applicability is undetermined. Statutory references sit in a separate Legal-owned obligations register, all marked candidate.

Running a provisional baseline while these are open is a deliberate choice. The alternative — building the full apparatus before producing any picture — leaves the institution unable to say anything about its AI maturity for as long as it takes, which is usually how the apparatus loses its funding.
